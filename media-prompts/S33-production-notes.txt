Technical qualifications and instructor Q&A:
1. Conventional APIs can also be slow, variable or billed by usage; analogy describes common tendencies, not a universal distinction.
2. Streaming can complicate moderating or validating partial output before display. For business-critical policy claims, consider buffering and verification before showing the claim; approved narration does not promise that streaming and validation always combine safely.
3. Tokens are not words; counts are illustrative, pricing and tokenization are provider/model specific. API cost can include other billable resources. Rate counts alone do not ensure spend ceilings; provider budgets can be alerts rather than hard caps.
4. Caching is only appropriate where response context, permissions, current information and user privacy permit it. Do not share personal answers across users.
5. A hallucination can occur despite successful HTTP response and valid JSON. Store generated history as generated content where policy permits; do not promote unverified claims to authoritative records.
6. Fallback example assumes core browsing and checkout do not depend on AI; resource isolation and healthy remaining dependencies matter. Retries may repeat billable operations.
7. External-provider scenario is illustrative; self-hosted models have different data boundaries. No assumption that providers train on customer data. Verify contracts, location, retention, access and lawful basis with stakeholders.
8. The lesson does not teach RAG, embedding search, prompt injection, model training, autonomous tool use, or full compliance implementation. S34 owns grounding/retrieval, S35 owns agent actions and permissions.
