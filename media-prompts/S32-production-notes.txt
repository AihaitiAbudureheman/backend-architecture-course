Technical review and caveats:
- Lesson outline S32 W29 problem: One client or bot can overwhelm the system, and the cloud bill. Verified against Final-Course-Outline-v4(1).pdf.
- RFC 6585 Section 4 defines HTTP 429 Too Many Requests; response MAY include Retry-After. It does not prescribe user identity or counting algorithm. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6585.html
- Envoy Gateway supports local and global rate limiting. Local is per proxy instance; global shares allowance across proxy instances. Actual configuration and availability vary by provider and deployment. https://gateway.envoyproxy.io/docs/concepts/rate-limiting/
- A limit of 100 requests per minute is illustrative, not a default. Arithmetic assumes the same identified client, one fixed-minute window, initially unused quota, no other admission restrictions, and enough incoming requests. Admitted requests are not guaranteed to complete successfully.
- Retry-After: 20 is illustrative; it does not guarantee success on a later attempt. Some products may omit the header.
- The diagram showing independent counters is a possible configuration, not a universal property of multi-instance gateways. Coordinated enforcement varies in precision, failure behavior and operational requirements.
- Shared limits can require supporting infrastructure; evaluate built-in capabilities before custom implementation.
- Authentication/authorization and IP identity are different. IP addresses can represent multiple users and users may change IP addresses.
- Request-count limits do not imply fixed spend ceilings or guaranteed aggregate backend capacity. Weighted cost and aggregate capacity limits may be necessary.
- 429 is typical, not mandatory for all overload and abuse scenarios; extreme abuse can be handled by dropping connections.
- Rate limiting is not a substitute for capacity planning, authentication, anti-abuse controls or budget limits.
- The supermarket analogy controls purchases, not time; the technical example introduces a time interval.
- Fixed window, sliding window, token bucket, shared-store atomicity, store failure policy, and vendor-specific configuration reserved for instructor Q&A.
